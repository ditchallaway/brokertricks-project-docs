## **Exercise \#6**

## **Create Your Badass Landing page For SaaS**

### **\#1 \- Map out the structure of your page**

Take the exact structure of the page I gave you in this book an write it on a document (template on the last page).

### **\#2 \- Re-use your answers**

Use the answers to your previous exercises as bullet points and write a very brief version of them on the document. **There’s an example in the template below.** 

### **\#3 \- Define the titles for the entire page**

Now that you have an idea of what should go on each section (header, problem, how it works…) start to define the titles for the subsections (things inside the header, problem, how it works...).

Make them short and specific, [here’s a great website](http://www.heynishi.com/) filled with good copy examples. Not necessarily for SaaS but you can still learn from most of the examples anyway.

### **\#4 \- Define the images**

If you have the titles defined you already know exactly what the content of the page would be so now it’s time to use images to help illustrate that.

Use images related to the titles you are using, it doesn’t have to be special if you can’t do custom images.

**Here are a few free resources:**

- [Undraw.co](https://undraw.co/): get 500 free illustrations to help you explain your points.  
- Use screenshots from the product (ideal when explaining the solution/”how it works” section). Use [screely](https://www.screely.com/) to make them stand out more.  
- Stock photos: I wouldn’t recommend these unless you tweak them and choose the right ones. It’s still a possibility tho.

### **\#5 \- Finish the copy**

Since your titles and images are already defined simply fill the text below the titles with something related. Use them to provide more context to what the titles explain.

The only people who read these are the ones who would be likely to have a question or objection regarding a certain point on the page so make sure to answer some of the objections identified in the respective exercise to help you come up with this copy.

## **Landing page template**

Header:

1. Describe what the product does in the title. Make simple and direct, you can use the problem it’s fixing on the title like “No more \<problem”, “Improve \<problem\>”, Want less \<problem\>?  
2. Very briefly introduce (one sentence) how the product achieves that.  
3. Have a clear and simple CTA (free trial or demo), that’s visible\! 

Initial Social proof:

- Logos of well-known companies in your niche.  
- Or stats with big numbers. Transferwise uses “Over £2 billion transferred every month” for example.

Problem:

1. Introduce the problem your fixing, the biggest one.  
2. Explain what happens if you don’t fix it in 1 or 2 points max.  
3. Explain why they can’t fix it themselves or why they didn’t couldn’t figure out the solution before.

How it works/Solution:

- For the title, you can use: “Here’s how X product can fix X problem”.  
- Use 3 \- 5 subsections explaining how the product fixes the problem on a high level.

Reinforcing benefits:

- What’s different about the product?  
- Is it faster, better, better support, easy integrations, seamless setup? Whatever unique benefits it brings to the table that makes it less of a commitment to switch.

More social proof:

- Use result driven testimonials ideally. E.g. “We improved X problem by 40%”.  
- Highlight the best parts of the testimonials if possible.  
- Make sure the potential customers can relate to the testimonials. If you’re targeting bloggers make all of your testimonials from bloggers if possible.  
- Don’t use generic stuff like “Awesome tool”, “Our team loves it”.  
- If you don’t have customers skip this step until you can get social proof. Alternatively, you can show the number of users that are (or were) on a free plan or trial.

Final CTA:

- Same CTA as the one on the header (free trial or demo most of the time).  
- Try to reduce the commitment with the copy. “The first 14 days are on us”, “Try for free”, “30-day money back guarantee” are just some examples.

Alternate CTA:

- Try to get an email address or at least make people stay on the website for longer.  
- Demos, lead magnets, articles, videos, more case studies or even feature pages.
