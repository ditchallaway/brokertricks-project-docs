   **I worked with veteran agents making a living in the field for years**  
   **I worked with rookie agents straight out of school with big dreams**   
   **I worked with hobby/part time agents. Occasional list a neighbors property or their own**  
   **I worked with luxury and resort property agents. Spend a lot on relationships, listing assets and marketing both client properties and themselves**  
   **i worked with broker/owner/agent working independently with 0 realtors under their license.**   
   **i worked with broker/owner/agent working with agents under them as well which is a fundamentally different business model**

**I would say that the area that their licence covered is a better predator of the type of listings they will list. As a general rule it's fair to say that newer agents lack the confidence to go after high ticket sellers regardless of land or residential. Ironically the sellers with less expensive offerings often are more difficult to accommodate in practice than someone selling a high ticket property generally has more experience and more comfortable with the process, has more money so relatively taking less risk.**

## **💸 Buying Behavior \+ Constraints**

7. **What was their typical marketing budget per listing?**  
   **As a general rule 10% to 20% of gross commissions should go to marketing. So if the listing price is $100,000 (exceedingly rare these days but good for example) then commissions would be $3,000 so marketing would be $300 to $600.**   
8. **Did they pay out of pocket or bill the seller/brokerage?**  
   **out of pocket**  
9. **What would they willingly pay $50–$150 for without blinking?**  
   **they would happily spend that amount to get the required images for the listing. Even unremarkable images, they would pay that amount just to not look foolish and have the seller nod in approval.**  
10. **What’s something they *never* wanted to pay for even if it helped?**  
    **open house. Agents claim that they don't usually lead to anything. Besides money the also take substantial time and effort. It's usually the seller that pushes for it without much evidence that it moves any meaningful needles. It's worth noting that signaling "work" to the seller is something that should be considered.**  
11. **What kind of results did they brag about? (e.g., fast sale, lots of calls, bidding war)**  
    **Speed. Often they brag about how quick they got it under contract. Closing deal is more outside their control.**  
12. **What made a seller think their agent was a pro? What signals mattered?**   
    **It depends on property type but an unexpected observation  is that higher value residential homes, the sellers, mostly women seemed to enjoy having a legitimate reason to show off their homes on social media guilt free status signals and care less about the sale itself or the metrics typically measured. The same probably holds true to lesser extent across all listing as a result listings being shared on sellers social media.**

## **🚩 Pain Points \+ Workflow Bottlenecks**

13. **Where did agents get stuck when creating listing visuals (maps, parcel info, branding, dimensions, layout)?**  
    **agents working under national brands have brand guidelines for color, aspect ratio, fonts etc. this represents a challenge for agents. The brands take full advantage of this charging high prices for assets hidden behind login screens where market forces can't drive competition and copyright doesn't permit 3rd parties to present alternatives. The agents themselves are able to use the brand assets to create marketing materials within the brand guidelines but in most cases lack the technical chops to pull it off. It's common for the exact same promotional item sold as generic print on demand yard signs for say $50 to be sold to agents by their national brand**

  **as a real estate yard sign  for 5 times that.** 

14. **What kind of listings were hard to explain or visualize to buyers?**  
    **if it's not on Google maps it's definitely hard to describe. Also complicated easements or access. If the neighboring land is forest service so won't be developed things like that.**  
15. **What mistakes did they repeatedly make in their marketing that cost them trust or time?**  
    **At no point during their education are agents primed on even the most basic marking principles. Too many calls to action. Fail to narrow the focus of a campaign enough to have impact or say anything to anyone at all. Tend to copy each other instead of evidence based testing so everyone and everything looks the same.**  
16. **How did they hand off tasks to assistants or TCs, if at all? What needed to be “agent-only”?**   
    **Agents use text messages and email. passing photos over sms compresses images yet look okay on small devices is an issue they have a hard time with understanding. Sending many  images over email un zipped or in the body is a pain for the receiver. They don't always understand zipped files and struggle to work with them on their phones. Multiple versions of files with undescriptive names is a thing as well. Agent only duties might be face to face with clients although many may only meet once and have their team do the rest. Most MLS have rules about sharing credentials but many agents ignore the rule out of necessity**

## **💡 Opportunity Sniff Test**

17. **Did anyone ever ask you, “Can you make this look better?” or “Is there a faster way to do this?” What were they talking about?**  
    **I can recall an instance when a a large parcel for sale was adjacent to a similar property listed by a competing office. The adjacent listing had been pre approved for subdivision but was not yet displayed as such by the title company. I was able to extract that plat layer from the pdf and place it over the neighboring listing in our images. Our parcel was under contract a day or two later. Proximity to other places was a common request e.g. schools public water access/boat launches.**   
18. **Which agents would have jumped at a tool that made them *look* more professional or modern?**   
    **Rookie agents would jump at a tool but often had no listings to use it on. More experienced agents would jump at a service that would handle it on their behalf.**  
19. **Did anyone ask for marketing help but never follow through — and if so, what blocked them?**  
    **Very few thought deeply about what a buyer's motivation might be and tended to rely on phrases like "build your dream home" even when it was pretty clear many were purchased to be flipped or rented out.**  
20. **Which agents *overpaid* for something you knew you could’ve done faster/better/cheaper?**

**Rookie agents tend to over prepare and overspend if they can afford it. Experienced agents who have enough clients to keep them busy tend to be using older workflows and simply pay someone to do the rest like social media posting.**
